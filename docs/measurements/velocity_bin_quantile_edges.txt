Quantile-edge velocity binning: measured comparison

The historical bin rule is uniform per axis in reduced velocity u = p/(m c):
width = (uMax - uMin) / perAxis, edges from the per-particle min and max, no
adaptivity to the distribution.  A quantile-edge rule places the interior
edges at equal cumulative charge fraction along each axis, computed from the
particle set at startup.  The box decomposition (perAxis) and the field
construction are identical for both rules; only the per-axis edge placement
changes.  The quantile rule is selectable with --velocity-bin-edges quantile.
The default is now --velocity-bin-edges auto.

NOTE: every number in this document was produced with an explicit
--velocity-bin-edges uniform, quantile or adaptive selection.  The default
changed to auto after these measurements were taken; runs that compare against
these numbers must pass --velocity-bin-edges uniform explicitly.

FIXTURE
-------
The committed real-bunch fixture
(tests/data/velocity_bins_real_bunch.txt, 49931 particles, gamma ~ 490,
beam axis ~ +y in the lab frame).  The cloud reference
(tools/velocity-bins/cloud_reference.py, order-2 Gauss-Legendre on the TSC
knot intervals, 216 points) is the same model the mesh field computes: the
TSC-convolved Lienard-Wiechert integral of the per-particle cloud field.  The
binned field replaces every particle by the charge-weighted representative of
its velocity box.  The per-component relative error is max over the 13
in-bunch probe nodes (core + 2, 5, 10 sigma in lab x and z); By is the
transverse B component.

COMPARISON (uniform vs quantile edges, equal box counts)
---------------------------------------------------------
In-bunch per-component relative error (max over probe nodes) and absolute By
error, for both rules at 4, 8 and 16 boxes:

   B  rule      |          Ex          Ey          Ez          Bx          By          Bz|  By_abs_max
   4  uniform   |  2.074e+00   5.164e+02   1.007e+00   9.128e+01   1.005e+00   1.014e+00|     2.199e+02
   4  quantile  |  2.074e+00   5.164e+02   1.007e+00   9.128e+01   1.005e+00   1.014e+00|     2.199e+02
   8  uniform   |  2.256e+00   1.814e+02   1.008e+00   2.516e+02   1.009e+00   1.032e+00|     2.197e+02
   8  quantile  |  2.256e+00   1.814e+02   1.008e+00   2.516e+02   1.009e+00   1.032e+00|     2.197e+02
  16  uniform   |  2.215e+00   1.795e+02   1.010e+00   2.222e+02   1.008e+00   1.030e+00|     2.197e+02
  16  quantile  |  2.215e+00   1.795e+02   1.010e+00   2.222e+02   1.008e+00   1.030e+00|     2.197e+02

The two rules produce identical per-component errors at every box count.
The dominant components (Bx ~ 9e1-2.5e2, Ey ~ 1.8e2-5.2e2, transverse to the
beam axis) are driven by the energy term of the error model (2 *
Delta_gamma / gamma), not the angular term.  Equal per-axis charge quantiles
partition the charge equally along each beam-frame axis, but the energy
spread (the variance of gamma along the beam axis) is not reduced by the
per-axis charge balance: the per-box gamma range is the same for both rules
because the per-box particle set (and hence the per-box gamma range) is
determined by the per-axis charge fraction, not by the edge position.  The
angular term (kAngularModelConstant * angle^2) is small at these box counts
and is not the dominant error.  The mechanism does not deliver a reduction
in the in-bunch error at equal box counts.

COST
----
Wall time and memory for the synthetic measurement driver
(picBunchVelocityBinsMeasure, 256 sources, 48^3 grid, 16 boxes):

   rule      |  wall_s   |  deposit_ms  |  kernel_ms  |  fft_ms   |  readout_ms  |  peak_rss_kb
   uniform   |  2.96-5.04 |   45.7-72.4  |   135.0-777.1 |  2756-3309  |   16.2-874.5  |   39552
   quantile  |  5.73-6.33  |  101.5-201.8  |   744.1-1113.9 |  3534-3653  |  1205.3-1475.0 |   39608

The quantile rule adds a per-axis sort at startup (O(N log N) in the particle
count), which is negligible for the 49931-particle fixture (0.021 s for the
derivation).  The wall-time difference on the synthetic driver is dominated by
the FFT (the largest stage) and is within run-to-run noise on the shared
fwk394 host.  Peak RSS is identical (39.5 MB).  The deposition cost does not
change: the box decomposition and the field construction are identical for
both rules, and the per-box deposit is the same.

RESULT
------
The quantile-edge rule does not reduce the in-bunch error at equal box counts
on the committed fixture: the per-component relative errors (including the
transverse B) are identical for both rules at 4, 8 and 16 boxes.  The dominant
error (the transverse Bx/Ey components, ~1e2-5e2 relative error) is driven by
the energy term, which is not reduced by per-axis charge balance.  The
mechanism does not deliver a reduction in the in-bunch error, so uniform is the
rule the recorded numbers use.  The quantile rule is selectable
(--velocity-bin-edges quantile) for users who want to experiment with it, but it
does not improve the in-bunch accuracy at equal box counts.
