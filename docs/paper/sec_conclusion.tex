\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\texttt{picBunch} \texttt{v0.9.2} computes the present-time field of a
uniformly moving, TSC-shaped bunch as a zero-padded free-space FFT
convolution with staggered Yee read-out, driven by a single TOML
configuration, and writes a uniform float32 openPMD checkpoint that restarts
under a stock PIConGPU, periodic and open-boundary, single- and multi-patch.
The release passes $18/18$ ctest with zero compiler warnings on a V100
\texttt{sm\_70}, an H100 \texttt{sm\_90}, and a gfx1100 HIP build.

The method is a far-field tool. In the far field the mesh is below
$3\times10^{-4}$ of the TSC-convolved continuum from $d=8$ cells for
$\gamma=1$, aspect 1, and from $d=32$ for $\gamma=1$, aspect 4; at
$\gamma=10$, aspect 1, the measured values are $6.5\times10^{-3}$ at
$d=32$ and $1.8\times10^{-3}$ at $d=48$. At $2\times10^{6}$ particles the
mesh-only CLI wall is $2.48$\,s on one H100 (compute $0.116$\,s),
deterministic (md5 \texttt{d3641d64\ldots}), and the cost is linear in the
particle count for the deposit and cubic in the padded grid for the FFT.
The opt-in near shell assembles exactly to roundoff and its fast backend
meets $10^{-3}$ stationary and moving axial, with the moving oblique
interior at $8.15\times10^{-2}$, the measured limit. The MPI compute is a
component-parallel mesh and a tile-parallel near shell, bitwise identical
at $1/2/4$ ranks, with the replicated deposit and source FFT capping the
mesh speedup at $1.20\times$ and the collective writer I/O-bound at four
ranks. The measured sub-cell limit at $\gamma\ge50$ is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:gamma-landscape}; in that regime the near shell is the
working tool.
